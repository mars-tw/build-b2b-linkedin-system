# Build a B2B LinkedIn System

## Operating Principle

Build an integrated system in which verified expertise creates buyer trust, trust creates relevant conversations, and conversations enter an owned follow-up process. Keep every recommendation specific to the user's evidence, buyers, market, and authority.

Read:

- [references/playbook.md](references/playbook.md) for domain frameworks and optical-industry examples
- [references/templates.md](references/templates.md) when producing a deliverable
- [references/quality-rubric.md](references/quality-rubric.md) before final delivery

## Select the Mode

Choose the narrowest mode that completes the request:

- `audit`: diagnose the current system and evidence gaps
- `design`: define positioning, profiles, content, ICP, CTA, and handoff
- `draft`: produce review-ready copy or messaging with visible evidence labels
- `operate`: execute only the explicitly authorized external actions

Combine modes when the user requests an end-to-end engagement. For a single post, use this skill for positioning and evidence, then follow any available company-specific publishing skill.

## Apply Precedence

Resolve conflicts in this order:

1. User instructions, company policies, and approved claims
2. Current verified source material
3. This skill's workflow and guardrails
4. Examples in the playbook

Never let an example override company evidence. Never transfer an optical-industry example into another sector as a fact.

## Maintain an Evidence Ledger

Classify material before drafting:

- `verified`: explicitly confirmed by a current authoritative source
- `supported`: present in supplied material but still awaiting owner review
- `hypothesis`: useful for planning only and labeled as an assumption
- `missing`: unavailable and prohibited from public claims

Track at least `claim`, `status`, `source`, `owner`, and `allowed use`. Distinguish factual verification from publication approval: a verified claim is not automatically approved for publishing.

## Enforce Guardrails

- Mark missing inputs instead of inventing specifications, certifications, customers, results, manufacturing capabilities, or observed prospect activity.
- Translate technical material into buyer-readable language without weakening accuracy.
- Optimize for buyer trust and qualified conversations, not vanity metrics or product-ad volume.
- Treat AI as a drafting aid. Require a knowledgeable human to verify claims, terminology, links, and tone before publication.
- Prefer relationship building and useful information over immediate selling. Do not automate spam, fake personalization, mass unsolicited outreach, or repeated unwanted follow-ups.
- Use only business-relevant prospect information and honor opt-outs or explicit disinterest.
- Obtain explicit authorization before publishing, messaging people, changing live profiles, or writing to a CRM.
- Verify current LinkedIn navigation, limits, and product behavior when exact platform instructions are requested.
- Respond in the user's language unless another output language is requested. Preserve accepted technical terms where translation would reduce precision.

## Intake

Use the intake brief in [references/templates.md](references/templates.md). Gather or infer only from supplied evidence:

- Company, products, markets, languages, and current LinkedIn assets
- Value proposition, technical advantages, applications, certifications, and proof
- Priority buyer industries, regions, company sizes, roles, needs, and decision authority
- Available employee spokespeople and subject-matter reviewers
- Desired CTA, destination links, CRM or handoff process, and success criteria
- Publication authority, review owner, constraints, and timeline

Proceed with labeled hypotheses when the task is exploratory. Stop for user input only when a missing fact would materially change a public claim, target audience, external action, or irreversible decision.

## Workflow

### 1. Audit the Current System

Audit five connected surfaces:

1. Company page: logo, banner, tagline, About, CTA, posts, employee links
2. Employee profiles: photo, headline, About, experience, skills, projects, recommendations
3. Content system: topic coverage, technical depth, proof, cadence, language, and review
4. Targeting: ICP clarity, search filters, mutual context, and interaction history
5. Conversion path: CTA destination, inquiry path, CRM capture, ownership, and follow-up

Return each item as `ready`, `needs evidence`, `missing`, or `blocked`. Attach evidence, business impact, and the smallest useful next action.

### 2. Define Positioning

Write the company position with:

- Who the company is
- What it makes or does
- Whom it serves
- What measurable or operational impact it creates

Build the company About section from value proposition, product lines, technical advantages, application domains, and verified certifications or quality evidence.

Write employee headlines as:

`role + professional domain + audience served + value created`

Reject copy that merely lists products without connecting them to a buyer problem, operational value, or proof.

### 3. Design the Company Page

Specify:

- Logo and banner concept that shows professional identity and real application context
- Tagline and About copy that state positioning and value
- CTA connected to a working website, inquiry form, business email, RFQ, event page, or technical download
- Ongoing posts and employee links that extend reach and trust

Align visual concepts with production, test equipment, and application environments when those are supported by source material.

### 4. Design Employee Profiles and Ambassadors

Prioritize owners or executives, sales or product managers, and technical or engineering experts. Tailor each profile and contribution to the person's real role.

Use the sequence:

1. Follow the company page
2. Share relevant company content with personal context
3. Add an authentic personal viewpoint
4. Join useful industry conversations

Do not impose identical scripts or engagement quotas that make employees appear coordinated or inauthentic.

### 5. Design the Content System

Select two to four content pillars according to ICP needs and available evidence:

- Industry insight
- Technical expertise
- Company culture and recruiting
- Customer or application cases

Create topics from verified inputs such as product trends, buyer problems, manufacturing processes, testing, reliability, applications, and supply-chain strengths.

For every topic, define the buyer question, source material, proof, author, CTA, and reviewer. Do not create a full calendar from weak or missing evidence.

Use this AI workflow:

1. Supply source material and keywords
2. Generate a structured draft
3. Translate technical material into buyer-readable language
4. Produce bilingual variants when useful
5. Have a subject-matter expert review and approve

### 6. Define the ICP and Prospect Research

Define industry, region, company size, role, buyer need, and decision authority. Convert those dimensions into keyword searches and filters.

Separate target-account criteria from contact-role criteria. Use mutual connections, relevant groups, and verified prior interactions to reduce cold-start friction. Record only meaningful, business-relevant interaction context.

### 7. Draft Relationship-First Outreach

Apply four rules:

1. Personalize with the recipient's company, role, work, or recent activity
2. Start a conversation before attempting development or selling
3. Offer a useful insight, resource, or problem-solving angle
4. Avoid pricing, pitching, or product dumping in the first message

Sequence outreach as `relevant interaction -> connection -> useful exchange -> permission-based follow-up -> owned handoff`. Keep placeholders explicit and never pretend to have observed activity that was not verified.

### 8. Map CTA and CRM Handoff

Connect each content or profile CTA to:

- a working destination
- a named response owner
- a response expectation
- a CRM or tracking stage
- the next permitted action

Flag any broken link, unowned inbox, untracked inquiry, or handoff without a follow-up rule.

### 9. Roll Out and Measure

Build a four-week sequence:

- Week 1: establish the company page and refresh brand presentation
- Week 2: optimize priority employee profiles and define the ambassador mechanism
- Week 3: publish the first reviewed content and begin relevant network expansion
- Week 4: connect interactions to CRM or another follow-up system

Before launch, measure evidence readiness, profile completion, reviewed assets, ICP completeness, CTA readiness, and owner assignment. After authorized launch, review relevant new connections, qualified-audience reach, message reply quality, inquiry progression, and opportunity movement.

Diagnose quality and commercial movement, not activity volume alone. Recommend changes only when the available observation window and sample are sufficient.

## Output Contract

Use the structures in [references/templates.md](references/templates.md). Deliver the smallest complete package needed for the selected mode. A full engagement should include:

1. Decision summary
2. Evidence ledger and unresolved claims
3. Current-state audit and prioritized gaps
4. Company positioning and page recommendations
5. Priority employee profile recommendations
6. Content pillars and evidence-backed calendar
7. ICP and prospect-research fields
8. Relationship-first outreach drafts
9. CTA and CRM handoff map
10. Thirty-day plan with owners, approval gates, and metrics

Label each external-facing asset as `draft`, `verified`, or `approved for publishing`. Do not label anything approved unless an authorized human has approved it.

## Final QA

- Score the deliverable with [references/quality-rubric.md](references/quality-rubric.md).
- Confirm every claim is represented correctly in the evidence ledger.
- Confirm the audience, problem, value, and CTA align.
- Confirm employee copy matches the person's actual role and experience.
- Confirm AI drafts received technical and brand review.
- Confirm outreach is personalized, useful, and non-pushy.
- Confirm every public or external action has explicit authorization.
- Confirm the 30-day plan assigns owners and closes the loop into follow-up.
- Do not deliver a full system with a critical failure in evidence integrity or external-action safety.
